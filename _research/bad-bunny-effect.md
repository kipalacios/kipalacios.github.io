brief:
  context: >
    The Super Bowl is one of the largest entertainment and media events in the world, and Bad
    Bunny's halftime performance sparked a wave of online discussion about Latino representation
    in mainstream entertainment. Moments like this shape cultural conversation — and PR and
    marketing teams use them to reach broader, more diverse audiences.
  purpose: >
    To measure how audiences responded online to Bad Bunny's appearance: whether sentiment was
    mostly positive, negative, or neutral, and what that reveals about the performance's cultural
    impact and audience engagement.

research_questions:
  - How did audiences respond online to Bad Bunny's appearance at the Super Bowl?
  - Was audience sentiment toward the performance mostly positive, negative, or neutral?
  - What was the overall sentiment of online conversation?

method:
  summary: >
    More than 90,000 reactions were collected through Mention using keywords around the
    performance, Latino culture, and representation, filtered to English and excluding video. A
    10,500-mention sample was analyzed in Orange for sentiment, emotion, topics, and key words.
  sample: >
    Before analysis the data was cleaned to cut noise — removing URLs, emojis, duplicates, and stop
    words, then lowercasing and tokenizing the text — to make the Tweet Profiler's lexicon-based
    emotion detection more reliable.
  formats:
    - Word cloud
    - Sentiment analysis
    - Emotion profiling
    - Key word analysis

findings:
  - label: Finding 01
    title: Overall sentiment leaned positive
    stats:
      - num: "48%"
        label: Positive mentions
    body: >
      Nearly half of all mentions were positive, outweighing negative sentiment and putting praise
      at the center of the conversation. A share of posts were likely misclassified by the
      profiler, so the split is read as a direction rather than an exact measure.
  - label: Finding 02
    title: The performance was polarizing
    stats:
      - num: "37%"
        label: Negative mentions
      - num: "14.5%"
        label: Neutral
    body: >
      Criticism was substantial, not marginal, and neutral commentary was rare. Audiences were
      emotionally invested either way — the performance sparked debate, not indifference.
  - label: Finding 03
    title: The conversation was social-first and high-volume
    stats:
      - num: "92K+"
        label: Mentions, mostly on Twitter/X
    body: >
      The reaction was immediate, opinion-driven, and highly shareable, with Twitter/X dominating
      the volume. That kind of real-time buzz is an opening for amplification and longer-term
      momentum.

recommendations:
  - title: Amplify the positive narrative
    body: >
      Highlight fan praise across owned and earned media, secure follow-up interviews on the
      performance's impact, and frame coverage around cultural significance — positioning it as a
      widely celebrated cultural moment.
  - title: Reframe polarization as cultural impact
    body: >
      Rather than countering criticism directly, lean into artistic boldness, representation, and
      global influence, and treat the debate as proof of relevance. Respond only to correct
      misinformation. The goal: shift the story from "divisive" to "impactful."
  - title: Turn buzz into sustained momentum
    body: >
      Deploy social-first content like clips and behind-the-scenes footage, engage influencers and
      fan communities, and package the engagement data for streaming, ticketing, and sponsorship
      conversations.

limitations:
  - Some posts were inaccurately profiled by the lexicon-based tool, which may have introduced outliers.
  - "Next steps: segment sentiment by audience type and platform, and analyze the themes behind negative sentiment."
  - "Track sentiment over time, link it to behavioral outcomes, and compare against benchmark performances."
