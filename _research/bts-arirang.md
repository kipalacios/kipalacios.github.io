brief:
  context: >
    BTS stepped back from group activities in 2022 for mandatory military service, which made
    their return one of the most anticipated comebacks in music. On March 20, 2026 they released
    ARIRANG, their fifth full-length studio album and an homage to their Korean roots — and fans
    immediately started debating the new sound and direction.
  purpose: >
    To analyze fan reactions to BTS's evolving sound, understand how fans define authenticity in
    the group's music, and examine how the tension between cultural representation and global
    commercial success shapes what audiences expect of artists.

research_questions:
  - What do fan reactions to BTS's evolving sound reveal about expectations of artists?
  - How do fan interpretations of BTS's music highlight tensions between cultural representation and global commercial success?

method:
  summary: >
    More than 90,000 English-language reactions were collected through Mention across social
    platforms (YouTube, video, and blogs excluded), using keywords for the group, the album, and
    each member. A 30,100-mention sample was analyzed in Orange for word frequency, emotion,
    topics, and semantic connections; a cleaner sample of under 5,000 was used for machine
    learning, where removing noise mattered most.
  formats:
    - Word cloud
    - Emotion profiling
    - LDA topic modeling
    - MDS
    - Semantic network
    - Supervised &amp; unsupervised ML

findings:
  - label: Finding 01
    title: Fans hold conflicting expectations of BTS as artists
    body: >
      Sentiment was mixed but highly engaged. Positive language ("like," "praise") sat alongside
      negative terms ("confusing," "argue"), with very little neutral language — a sign of strong
      emotional investment rather than passive listening. Some fans celebrated experimentation and
      global success; others worried about the change in musical style. Fans weren't just reviewing
      an album, they were negotiating how they think artists should evolve.
  - label: Finding 02
    title: Authenticity vs. globalization is the core tension
    body: >
      Topic modeling surfaced "western," "kpop," and "global" again and again. Fans disagree on
      what authenticity even means: for some it's holding on to Korean cultural roots, for others
      it's the ability to adapt and succeed worldwide. That turns every musical change into a
      judgment about cultural meaning and commercial strategy, not just sound.
  - label: Finding 03
    title: Perception is shaped across platforms
    body: >
      Frequent mentions of "Netflix," "documentary," "comeback," and "live" showed that fans read
      the music and the media around it together. Behind-the-scenes and documentary content is
      where many fans go to understand the group's artistic direction.

recommendations:
  - title: Address diverse fan expectations
    body: >
      Pair experimental, global tracks with songs that reflect BTS's earlier style, and use Weverse,
      live streams, and comeback interviews to explain the intent behind stylistic changes —
      speaking directly to the confusion that showed up in fan discussion.
  - title: Balance cultural identity with global appeal
    body: >
      Build Korean-language songs and cultural elements into globally targeted releases, and carry
      that identity through visual concepts, music videos, and performances.
  - title: Use media content to shape perception
    body: >
      Expand documentaries and behind-the-scenes content, and time it with music launches so fans
      have context for creative decisions as they hear them.

limitations:
  - Technical issues in Orange limited parts of the analysis.
  - A larger, more representative sample would strengthen the findings.
  - A more rigorous filtering procedure would further reduce noise in the data.
